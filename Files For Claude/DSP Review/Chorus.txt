To achieve the professional, immersive sound of stock FL Studio chorus effects for **Vibesynth**, you must move beyond a single modulated delay line. High-end chorus depends on **stereo decorrelation**, **multi-voice density**, and **targeted frequency processing**.

Here is the professional breakdown for the **ChorusDSP**.

---

### 1. The Crossover Stage (Targeted Routing)
A common mistake in digital chorus is modulating the low-end, which causes "phase mud" and ruins the punch of your kick or bass.
* **The Logic:** Use a **Linkwitz-Riley 4th Order crossover** (24dB/oct). 
* **Processing:** Only the High-Pass band (frequencies above your `Cross Cutoff`, usually 200Hz–500Hz) should enter the chorus engine. The Low-Pass band remains "dry" and mono-stable, then sums back at the end.

### 2. Multi-Voice Architecture
A "pro" chorus isn't just one voice; it’s a cluster. 
* **The Logic:** Use **3 independent voices** per channel (6 total for stereo).
* **The Math:** Each voice should have a slightly different base `Delay` time (e.g., Voice A = 10ms, Voice B = 14ms, Voice C = 18ms). This prevents the "metallic" resonant peaks that occur when multiple voices overlap perfectly.

### 3. The LFO Engine (Multi-Sine & Stereo Phase)
Instead of a single sine wave, use a "Multi-Sine" generator.
* **The Shape:** Sum two sines together (e.g., `primary_rate` and `primary_rate * 0.37`) to create a more "organic" and less predictable wobble.
* **Stereo Phase (The Width Secret):** This is the `Stereo` parameter (0 to 360 degrees).
    * **Left LFO:** $\sin(\phi)$
    * **Right LFO:** $\sin(\phi + \text{StereoDegrees})$
    * *Result:* At 180°, the left voice is moving "in" while the right is moving "out," creating massive perceived width.

### 4. High-Quality Interpolation
If the chorus sounds "grainy," it is due to linear interpolation errors in the delay line.
* **Requirement:** You **must** use **Cubic Lagrange Interpolation** for the delay line read pointers. This ensures the pitch-shifting (which is essentially what a chorus is) remains liquid and smooth.
