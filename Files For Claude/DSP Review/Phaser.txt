To make the **PhaserDSP** for **Vibesynth** sound like a professional, high-end hardware unit rather than a simple digital sweep, you must focus on the **All-Pass Cascade architecture** and **Phase Decorrelation**. 

A phaser works by creating "notches" in the frequency spectrum through phase cancellation. The more stages you have, the more notches you get, and the more complex the sound becomes.

---

### 1. The All-Pass Cascade Architecture
Unlike a Chorus or Flanger that uses delay lines, a Phaser uses a series of **All-Pass Filters**.
* **The Logic:** You need a dynamic array of filters. High-end phasers (like the ones in FL) allow you to switch between 4, 12, or even 24 stages. 
* **Stage Count:** Each "pair" of stages creates one notch. A 24-stage phaser creates 12 deep notches for a very "liquidy" and vocal-like sweep.
* **The Component:** Use `juce::dsp::FirstOrderTPTFilter` set to All-Pass mode. The TPT (Topology Preserving Transform) structure is critical here because it remains stable even when the LFO is sweeping the frequency at high speeds.

### 2. The LFO & Mapping (Sweep Range)
The LFO shouldn't just move between 0 and 1; it needs to sweep the **cutoff frequency** of all all-pass filters simultaneously.
* **Mapping:** Map the LFO (0.0 to 1.0) to a frequency range (e.g., 200Hz to 12kHz) using a **Logarithmic** scale. Human hearing is logarithmic, so a linear sweep will sound like it spends too much time in the high frequencies and "teleports" through the lows.
* **Stereo Phase (The Width Secret):** Just like the Chorus, offset the Right channel LFO phase by the `Stereo` parameter (0–360°). At 180°, the notches in the Left ear go up while the notches in the Right ear go down, creating massive spatial movement.

### 3. Feedback (The "Resonance" Stage)
Feedback is what makes a phaser "whistle" or sound "metallic."
* **The Logic:** Take the output of the **last** active stage in the cascade, multiply it by the `Feedback` amount, and inject it back into the **first** stage.
* **Phase Inversion:** Give the user an "Invert Feedback" toggle. Inverting the feedback (multiplying by -1.0) shifts the notches, often resulting in a "hollower" or "chunkier" sound.

### 4. Logic for Dynamic Stages
To allow the user to change the number of stages (e.g., 4, 8, 12, 24) without audio glitches:
* **The Implementation:** Always keep 24 filters in memory. Only loop through and process the number of filters specified by the `Stages` parameter. When the user changes the count, use a `juce::SmoothedValue` to crossfade the output of the new "last stage" to prevent pops.
