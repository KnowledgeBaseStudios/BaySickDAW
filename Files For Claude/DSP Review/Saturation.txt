### Module 1: The "Fruity" Saturation Engine
The goal here is to replicate the behavior of a high-end waveshaper that feels "analog" but stays clean. This is the breakdown of the logic you should hand to Claude.

#### 1. Pre-Processing (The "Tone" Stage)
Before the distortion hits, you need a **Pre-Emphasis Filter**.
* **Logic:** A high-shelf filter at 5kHz.
* **Why:** Pushing high frequencies into the saturator creates that "shimmer" or "air." If you saturate a flat signal, the low end often gets "farty."
* **Formula:** Use `juce::dsp::IIR::Coefficients<float>::makeHighShelf`.

#### 2. The Core Shaper (Asymmetrical Sigmoid)
Instead of standard `tanh`, use this asymmetrical function. It mimics the way a vacuum tube or a tape head saturates one side of the waveform slightly differently than the other, creating musical even-order harmonics.

**The Math:**
$$f(x) = \frac{x + (k \cdot x^2 \cdot \text{sgn}(x))}{1 + |x|}$$
* **$x$:** Input sample (post-pre-gain).
* **$k$:** The "Flowers" or "Symmetry" amount (0.0 to 1.0).
* **$\text{sgn}(x)$:** Returns 1 if positive, -1 if negative (maintains the direction of the wave).

#### 3. Oversampling (The "Pro" Requirement)
You cannot run this at 44.1kHz or it will sound like "crap" due to aliasing.
* **Requirement:** 4x Oversampling. 
* **Implementation:** Wrap the shaper in a `juce::dsp::Oversampling` block.
    1.  **Upsample** 4x.
    2.  **Process** the math above.
    3.  **Downsample** back to the host rate.

#### 4. Post-Processing (The "Clean-up")
To ensure it doesn't sound "digital" or "broken":
* **DC Offset Filter:** A one-pole High Pass Filter at 5Hz.
    * $y[n] = x[n] - x[n-1] + 0.995 \cdot y[n-1]$
* **Post-Gain Compensation:** Automatically reduce the output volume by the same amount you boosted the input gain (Sensitivity).
