The **LimiterDSP** is the final guard for your master chain in **Vibesynth**. A professional limiter is essentially a specialized compressor with an infinite ratio, a "ceiling," and—most importantly—**Look-Ahead** logic. Without look-ahead, a digital limiter will always let fast transients "peak" before it can react.

Here is the professional breakdown for the **Peak Limiter**.

---

### 1. The Look-Ahead Buffer
To catch every single peak, the limiter needs to "see the future." 
* **The Logic:** You must implement a `juce::dsp::DelayLine` for the main audio path. 
* **The Timing:** A standard professional look-ahead is between **1.0ms and 5.0ms**.
* **How it works:** While the main audio is sitting in the delay buffer, the sidechain (detector) is analyzing the *incoming* (non-delayed) signal. By the time a massive peak exits the delay buffer, the gain reduction has already smoothly ramped down to meet it.

### 2. The Gain Computer (Hard Ceiling)
The goal is to ensure the signal never exceeds the `Ceiling` (usually -0.1dB).
* **The Formula:** $$GainTarget = \min(1.0, \frac{Ceiling_{linear}}{PeakLevel_{linear}})$$
* **The Logic:** If the peak level is below the ceiling, gain is 1.0 (no change). If it's above, you calculate exactly how much to turn it down to hit the ceiling perfectly.

### 3. Release Curves (The "Transparency" Secret)
The "crap" sound in limiters usually comes from a release that is too static, causing "pumping." Professional limiters use a **Two-Stage Release**:
1.  **Fast Release:** Handles short transients (snaps/clicks) quickly so they don't dull the overall sound.
2.  **Slow Release:** Handles sustained loud sections (bass notes) more gracefully to prevent low-end distortion.
* **Adaptive Release:** Make the release time depend on the amount of gain reduction. If you're cutting 10dB, the release should slow down to avoid "squashing" the life out of the track.

### 4. True Peak Detection (Inter-Sample Peaks)
Standard digital meters only look at sample points. However, when converting back to analog, the curve between samples can "overshoot" 0dB.
* **The Logic:** Use **4x Oversampling** for the *detector path only*. 
* **The Result:** By detecting peaks at 4x the sample rate, you catch "Inter-Sample Peaks" (ISP) that would otherwise cause clipping on consumer speakers or Spotify/YouTube transcodes.
