To make your **DelayDSP** sound like a high-end, professional studio unit rather than a simple digital echo, you must implement **fractional interpolation**, **state-variable filtering within the feedback loop**, and **asymmetrical waveshaping**. 

Standard "math-correct" delays sound thin because the feedback is just a copy of the input. Pro delays sound better because every time the sound repeats, it degrades—losing high-end and gaining subtle harmonic saturation.

---

### 1. The Core Architecture (Fractional Delay)
To allow for smooth time changes and "Wow/Flutter" modulation without digital clicks or "zipper noise," you cannot use integer-based delay lines.
* **Requirement:** Use **Cubic Lagrange Interpolation**. This allows you to read between samples (e.g., at sample index 44.1), keeping the pitch-shifting liquid and smooth.
* **The Logic:** Use `juce::dsp::DelayLine<float, juce::dsp::DelayLineInterpolationType::cubic>`.

### 2. The Feedback Loop (The "Degradation" Chain)
The secret to a "lush" delay is what happens *inside* the feedback loop before the signal is written back into the buffer. This must happen in this exact order:
1.  **High-Pass/Low-Pass Filters:** Use the **Zavalishin TPT (Topology Preserving Transform)** model. This ensures that even when you modulate the filter cutoff, it doesn't "pop" or lose phase alignment.
2.  **Saturation Stage:** Apply the **Asymmetrical Sigmoid** we discussed in the Saturation module. This ensures that feedback at high levels (near self-oscillation) sounds like "warm analog warmth" instead of "harsh digital clipping."
3.  **DC Offset Filter:** A 5Hz High-Pass filter to prevent the feedback loop from building up a silent DC voltage that eats your headroom.

### 3. Time Modulation (The "Analog" Drift)
Static delays sound artificial. To add "life," you need a dedicated LFO modulating the delay time.
* **Wow & Flutter:** Use a slow Sine LFO (0.1Hz – 1.0Hz) to subtly shift the delay time by ±2ms. 
* **Keep Pitch Logic:** If the user changes the delay time, you have two choices:
    * **Tape Mode:** The pitch warps as the delay time changes (standard for FL-style).
    * **Digital Mode:** Crossfade between two delay lines to change time without pitch shifting.

### 4. Diffusion (The "Cloud" Effect)
To make the delay feel more like a reverb (a "cloud" of sound), you need a diffusion network.
* **The Logic:** Place 2 to 4 **All-Pass filters** at the very end of the feedback loop. 
* **Result:** This "smears" the echoes over time, so the 4th or 5th repeat sounds more like a wash of sound than a distinct tap.
