To make the **FlangerDSP** for **Vibesynth** sound professional—specifically mimicking the "metallic" but smooth character of FL Studio’s stock effects—you need to focus on **high-order interpolation**, **feedback damping**, and **phase-inverted summing**.

A flanger is essentially a chorus with much shorter delay times and a heavy emphasis on the feedback loop, which creates the "comb filter" effect.

---

### 1. The Short-Delay Architecture
While a chorus lives in the 10ms–30ms range, a professional flanger lives in the **0.1ms to 10ms** range. 
* **The Logic:** At these extremely short times, the peaks and notches of the comb filter are spaced far apart, creating that recognizable "whoosh."
* **Interpolation:** Because you are modulating the delay time at sub-millisecond levels, **Cubic Lagrange Interpolation** is mandatory. Without it, the "sweep" will sound stepped or grainy.

### 2. The Feedback Loop & Damping
The "metallic" ring of a flanger comes from the feedback. However, infinite digital feedback sounds harsh and "cheap."
* **Damping Filter:** Place a **One-Pole Low Pass Filter** inside the feedback loop (the `DAMP` parameter). This simulates the high-frequency absorption of analog circuits, making the feedback sound "warm" even at high levels.
* **Feedback Inversion:** Implement a toggle to invert the feedback signal (multiply by -1.0). 
    * **Positive Feedback:** Creates "Jet" flanging (peaks at harmonics).
    * **Negative Feedback:** Creates "Hollow" or "Tubular" flanging (notches at harmonics).

### 3. The LFO Shape Morphing
Stock FL effects often allow you to change the "weight" of the sweep.
* **The Logic:** Instead of just a Sine wave, implement an LFO that can morph between a **Sine** and a **Triangle** wave.
* **Why:** A Sine wave spends more time at the "top" and "bottom" of the sweep, while a Triangle wave moves at a constant speed. Morphing between them allows the user to dial in how "dizzy" the effect feels.

### 4. The Output Matrix (Stereo Width)
To get that "wide" professional sound, you need to manipulate the phase of the wet and dry signals separately.
* **Phase Offset:** Offset the Right LFO by the `Phase` parameter (0–360°).
* **Cross-Mixing:** Implement a `Cross` parameter that takes the Wet signal from the Left channel and mixes it into the Right channel output (and vice-versa). This creates a complex stereo image that feels like the sound is "spinning" around the listener's head.
