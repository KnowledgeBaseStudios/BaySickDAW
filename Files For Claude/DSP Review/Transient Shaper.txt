To make your **TransientShaperDSP** sound like a professional, punchy FL-style plugin rather than a simple volume gate, the secret lies in **frequency-dependent detection** and **envelope difference modeling**. 

Standard transient shapers often "pump" the low-end in an ugly way. For **Vibesynth**, we will use a split-band architecture that preserves the sub-fundamental while reshaping the "snap."

### 1. The Frequency Split (Linkwitz-Riley)
You don't want to shape the sub-bass of a kick drum the same way you shape the "click" of the beater. 
* **The Logic:** Use a **Linkwitz-Riley 4th Order crossover** (24dB/oct). This is the "Gold Standard" because it is phase-perfect at the summing point. 
* **Split Point:** Fixed or adjustable around **260Hz - 350Hz**. 
* **Routing:** The Low band bypasses the shaper entirely to keep the weight of the track consistent. Only the High band is processed.

### 2. The Detection Logic (The "Difference" Method)
A professional shaper works by comparing two envelope followers: one very fast (Attack) and one slower (Sustain/Release).

**The Math:**
1.  **Fast Envelope ($Env_{fast}$):** Follows the signal peaks almost instantly (e.g., 1ms–5ms).
2.  **Slow Envelope ($Env_{slow}$):** Follows the signal more gracefully (e.g., 20ms–50ms).
3.  **The Transient Signal:** $Transient = \frac{Env_{fast}}{Env_{slow} + \epsilon}$ (where $\epsilon$ is a tiny floor value).

If the signal is a steady tone, $Env_{fast}$ and $Env_{slow}$ are nearly equal, and the result is 1.0 (no change). If a sharp snare hit happens, $Env_{fast}$ spikes while $Env_{slow}$ lags, creating a value > 1.0. This is your "Attack" trigger.

### 3. Ballistics (Time Constants)
To avoid "clicking" sounds, you must smooth the gain changes.
* **Attack Phase:** Use a **Logarithmic** curve for the boost.
* **Release Phase:** Use an **Exponential** curve for the "Sustain" part of the shaper. 
* **Formula:** $\alpha = 1.0 - e^{-1.0 / (time\_ms \cdot 0.001 \cdot fs)}$

### 4. Output Saturation (The Soft Clipper)
Transient shaping often creates massive peaks that will clip your DAW's master. 
* **The Logic:** Place a **Soft Clipper** (Asymmetrical Sigmoid) at the end of the high-band chain.
* **Pro Tip:** Use the **4x Oversampling** logic we discussed for the Saturation module here. This ensures that when you "boost" an attack, any resulting clipping is "warm" and analog-sounding rather than "digital crunch."
