To make the **ReverbDSP** for **Vibesynth** sound professional and "lush" like stock FL or high-end algorithmic reverbs, you must avoid simple delay-tap models. You need a **Feedback Delay Network (FDN)** architecture. This allows for high-density diffusion without the metallic ringing associated with cheaper reverbs.

Here is the full breakdown for the professional Algorithmic Reverb.

---

### 1. The Input Stage (Early Reflections & Predelay)
Before the sound enters the "wash" of the late reverb, it needs physical cues to define the room's size.
* **Predelay:** A simple `juce::dsp::DelayLine`. If **Tempo Sync** is on, calculate the delay based on the host BPM.
* **Early Reflections (ER):** This is a separate branch of 6–10 short, non-modulated delay taps. The spacing should be irregular (prime numbers) to prevent resonance.
    * *Pro Tip:* Low-pass filter the ER branch more aggressively than the Late branch to simulate wall absorption.

### 2. The Late Reverb Core: The FDN Matrix
An FDN uses a bank of parallel delay lines that feed back into each other through a **Unitary Matrix**. 
* **The Matrix:** Use a **Householder Matrix**. This ensures that the total energy is preserved as it circulates, which prevents the reverb from "fading too fast" or "exploding."
    * Formula for a 4x4 Householder: $Matrix = I - \frac{2}{N} \cdot [1...1]^T \cdot [1...1]$
* **Prime Delay Times:** The lengths of the delay lines in the FDN *must* be prime numbers (in samples) to ensure the echoes don't align and cause "ringing."

### 3. Dual-Band Decay (The "Bass" Parameter)
One of your requirements is a **BASS** multiplier for the decay. This is achieved by placing a crossover in the feedback loop.
* **Logic:** Inside the feedback loop (after the matrix), split the signal with a Linkwitz-Riley crossover at the **CROSS** frequency.
* **Processing:**
    * **Low Band:** Multiply by $DecayTime \times Bass$.
    * **High Band:** Multiply by $DecayTime$.
* **Damping:** Apply an additional one-pole Low-Pass filter to the High Band, controlled by the **DAMP** parameter.

### 4. Diffusion & Stereo Width
To make the reverb sound "smooth" rather than "choppy," you need **All-pass Diffusers** at the input of the FDN.
* **Diffuser Chain:** 4 all-pass filters in series with very short delay times (5ms–20ms). These "smear" the initial transient.
* **Stereo Separation (SEP):** Use a Mid/Side (M/S) matrix at the very end.
    * To increase width: $Side = Side \times SEP$.
    * To decrease (Mono): $Side = Side \times (1.0 - SEP)$.

### The Instructions for Claude
When you hand off the `Reverb.txt` file to Claude, provide these specific technical "Golden Rules":

> "Rewrite the `ReverbDSP` class using a **4x4 Feedback Delay Network (FDN)** architecture:
> 1. **Matrix:** Use a **Householder Matrix** for the feedback loop to ensure energy stability.
> 2. **Delay Lines:** Use prime-number sample lengths for the FDN delay lines to prevent metallic ringing.
> 3. **Diffusion:** Place a chain of 4 **All-pass Filters** at the input stage to smear transients before they enter the FDN.
> 4. **Dual-Band Decay:** Implement a crossover (Linkwitz-Riley) inside the feedback loop. Apply the `DEC` time to the high band and `DEC * BASS` to the low band.
> 5. **Stereo Logic:** Implement a **Mid/Side (M/S) processing** stage at the output to control the `SEP` (Stereo Separation) parameter.
> 6. **Interpolation:** Use **Cubic Interpolation** for any modulated delay lines to avoid digital artifacts."
