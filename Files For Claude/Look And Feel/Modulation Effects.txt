## **Pattern 1: The "Dual-Layer Composite" Knob**
*Found in "Effects 1.jpg" and "Modulation Effects.jpg"*

### **Visual Components**
* **Base Layer (The Skirt):** A wide, glossy black circle. It needs a subtle radial gradient to simulate a domed top. The outer edge has a sharp, fine-toothed knurling pattern (vertical ridges).
* **Top Layer (The Cap):** A smaller, silver/aluminum circle sitting directly in the center. 
* **The "Concentric" Effect:** The silver cap requires a texture made of hundreds of very thin, faint circular lines to simulate turned metal.
* **The Indicator:** A single, razor-thin white line etched onto the black skirt.

### **Functional & Interaction Logic**
* **Decoupled Rotation:** You can script this so only the *skirt* (with the white line) rotates, while the *silver cap* remains static, or vice versa. Usually, the whole assembly rotates, but having the cap static is a common "high-end" UI trick.
* **Shadow Scaling:** The drop shadow should be slightly offset (e.g., bottom-right). For added realism, the shadow's blur radius should increase slightly as the user "grabs" the knob (simulating pulling it toward them).

## **Pattern 2: The "Asymmetric Pointer" (Chicken Head)**
*Found in "Dynamics & Transient Processing.png"*

### **Visual Components**
* **The Silhouette:** An asymmetric, aerodynamic shape. One side is a sharp "beak" (the pointer), the other is a heavy, rounded counterweight.
* **Material:** High-gloss black plastic. This requires strong "specular highlights"—bright white spots on the edges of the beak and the top of the dome.
* **The Marker:** A crisp white line running from the center of the dome to the tip of the beak.
* **The Static Base:** A hexagonal "bolt" or nut drawn on the panel *behind* the knob that does not rotate.

### **Functional & Interaction Logic**
* **Angular Snapping:** This control is often used for selectors. Implement logic that "snaps" the rotation to specific degree intervals (e.g., every 30°) rather than moving smoothly.
* **Ballistic Resistance:** Script a "heavy" feel where the cursor moves further than the knob rotates, giving the impression of mechanical resistance.

## **Pattern 3: The "Interlocking" Radio Stack**
*Found in the 'Ratio' section of "Effects 1.jpg"*

### **Visual Components**
* **The Housing:** A recessed vertical trench in the panel.
* **The Buttons:** Rectangular matte-black blocks. 
* **The 3D State:** The "Off" state is slightly raised with a highlight on the top edge. The "On" (active) state is physically recessed, shifting the shadow to the *inside* top edge to look "pushed in."

### **Functional & Interaction Logic**
* **Exclusive vs. Multi-Mode:** * **Standard:** Only one button can be active. Pressing one pops the others out.
    * **"Shift-Override":** Implement logic where holding a modifier key (like Shift or Alt) allows the user to toggle multiple buttons "In" at once, overriding the standard radio-group behavior.
